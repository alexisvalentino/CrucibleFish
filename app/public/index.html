What this workbench does

One AI guide, 100 synthetic customers, and three time floors — Day 1, Day 14, and Day 30. You describe a startup; the workbench pressure-tests it and puts the reasoning beside a conversation you can interrogate.

Read this first: every number here is a synthetic hypothesis, not measured customer research, a revenue forecast, or verified market sizing. Use it to design sharper real-world tests — not to skip them. The banner at the top links to the full limits.

Connect an AI provider for live runs

Live simulations and guide replies use the provider configured under AI connections in the header.

  • OpenAI API key — paste it here, or configure a server key.
  • Response model drives the critic and guide. Fast swarm model drives the five 20-person batches; a cheaper model keeps runs affordable.
  • Custom base URL is optional, for an OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
  • Remember keys in this browser is opt-in. Keys go only to the local server and are never written into run files.

No key yet? Explore demo uses an illustrative example and makes no paid calls.

Build a validation run

Choose Start with an idea or open New simulation from the navigation.

  1. Start with a name, a first customer, and a one-line pitch. Those three details are enough.
  2. Website, business model, geography, pricing, traction, research notes, and current features are optional. Add them when you have them.
  3. The baseline simulates your idea as described. Future plans stay out of the baseline and can be tested later if you want.
  4. Choose Run simulation to model 100 synthetic customer journeys across 30 days.

Want to see a filled brief? Choose Fill with PawsMatch example.

Find your way around

  • Conversation — ask the guide about results, about a customer by number or name, or to challenge an assumption. It is a reasoning partner, not an oracle.
  • Simulation — three floors and the 100-customer grid. The baseline keeps your idea unchanged; testing a future change is optional.
  • Venture critique — feature triage, the four-stage data flywheel, and the 90-day plan.
  • Market & capital — market graph, sizing hypotheses, and an investor shortlist. Verify all of it externally.

Test changes when you want

Simulations generate options; real people decide what is true.

  1. Run the unchanged baseline first. Test a future change only when you have one you want to explore.
  2. Run a few variations and keep patterns that stay stable; ignore numbers that swing wildly.
  3. Ask the guide to challenge your assumptions, not to predict revenue.
  4. Validate important claims with interviews, product analytics, and primary sources before committing time or money.
This stays a local, inspectable workbench by design. ROADMAP.md describes the next experiments. One last thing before you start.

Name your guide

This is the only conversational persona in the workbench — one persistent guide across every run and conversation. Give them a name that feels like yours; you can change everything later from the guide card in the sidebar.

The guide fronts the simulation but is never one of the 100 customers, and never claims to be a real person, investor, or advisor.

What these results are — and are not

An honest boundary for every figure on this screen.

Synthetic by construction

CrucibleFish creates 100 synthetic customer profiles from your brief and a fixed archetype distribution. No real customer was observed or interviewed. The PawsMatch demo's retention and GMV figures are hardcoded illustrations.

Not a forecast

Day 1, Day 14, and Day 30 are written in a single model pass. Retention, willingness to pay, GMV, market sizing, and investor matches are model-generated hypotheses — not measured outcomes, validated research, or investor interest.

What it is good for

Surfacing friction, payer confusion, and scope traps early; stress-testing the idea as described; and turning vague assumptions into specific questions. You can optionally compare a future change. Treat every result as a prompt for a real test, not the answer.

Getting closer to the truth

Add real evidence in the optional seed-material field, run several variations and look for stable patterns, then validate with real interviews, product analytics, and primary sources before committing time or money.

Data and keys

Runs stay local. If you configure a provider, your brief is sent to that endpoint; keys go only to the local server and are never written into run files. No Zep or OASIS service is used.
